Late filing and payment

I missed a BIR deadline. What could I have to pay?

A late return can create more than one charge. The usual starting formula is basic tax due + surcharge + interest + any applicable compromise penalty. The exact amount depends on the return, taxpayer classification, due date, payment date, and whether the case involves ordinary delay, willful neglect, or fraud.

This is not a penalty calculator. Do not pay from this page alone. Confirm the return, dates, taxpayer classification, and current BIR rules. Back taxes, several missed periods, notices, audits, or disputed assessments should be reviewed by a Philippine CPA or tax lawyer.

The possible components

1. Basic tax

The unpaid amount itself

The tax originally due does not disappear because the deadline passed.

2. Surcharge

A percentage added to the tax

Ordinary late filing or payment may carry a civil surcharge. More serious findings can carry a higher surcharge.

3. Interest and compromise

Time-based and administrative additions

Interest can run from the due date until payment. A compromise amount may also apply depending on the violation and current schedule.

Frequently asked questions

What is the usual surcharge for ordinary late filing or late payment?

Section 248 of the National Internal Revenue Code generally provides a 25% civil penalty in common cases such as filing after the deadline, paying after the deadline, failing to pay a deficiency assessment on time, or failing to pay the full amount shown on a return. Qualifying micro and small taxpayers may be entitled to a reduced 10% civil penalty under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act. Classification and eligibility must be confirmed.

When can the surcharge be higher?

A 50% surcharge may apply in cases involving willful neglect to file or a false or fraudulent return. Do not self-classify a mistake as fraud—or assume it is harmless. Obtain professional advice if records are incomplete, income was omitted, or the BIR has issued a notice.

How is interest added?

Interest may run on unpaid tax from the statutory due date until full payment. The applicable rate is tied to the legal interest framework in the Tax Code. The Ease of Paying Taxes Act provides a reduced rate for qualifying micro and small taxpayers. Because rates and classifications matter, BetterBuwis does not yet compute interest automatically.

What is a compromise penalty?

It is an amount the BIR may use to settle certain violations without pursuing criminal prosecution, subject to the applicable schedule and authority. It is separate from the basic tax, surcharge, and interest. The amount can depend on the type of return, tax due, delay, and violation.

Can I be penalized even when the return shows zero tax due?

Yes. A filing obligation can exist even when no tax is payable. A registered tax type may require a periodic or zero return until the registration is formally updated or closed. Late or non-filed information and zero returns may still attract penalties or open cases.

What if I filed on time but paid late?

Late payment can still trigger surcharge and interest even when the return itself was submitted by the deadline. Keep the filing confirmation and payment reference because they establish different dates.

What if I paid only part of the amount shown?

The unpaid balance may continue to attract applicable additions. Partial payment does not necessarily stop all consequences. Preserve official payment references and seek a computation based on the actual dates and remaining balance.

What if I missed several quarterly and annual returns?

Each required return and filing period can be treated separately. A year of inactivity does not automatically erase obligations attached to an active registration. First obtain a list of registered tax types and filed returns; then reconcile period by period before paying anything.

Do micro and small taxpayers receive reduced penalties?

The Ease of Paying Taxes Act introduced reduced civil penalties and interest treatment for qualifying micro and small taxpayers, plus reduced amounts for certain information-return, invoicing, and registration violations. Do not assume the reduction applies until your classification and the particular violation are confirmed.

Can I fix a missed filing voluntarily?

Often the safest first move is to identify the correct return, verify whether it was already filed, compute the tax and additions using official rules, then file and pay through an available authorized channel. Voluntary correction does not guarantee that every penalty is waived, but delaying further can increase interest and complexity.

Do I always have to appear at an RDO?

No. Filing and payment options vary by return, registration status, and system access. Some steps can be completed through eBIRForms, eFPS, authorized agent banks, or electronic payment channels. Other registration corrections, open cases, or account mismatches may still require RDO action. Check the current official BIR instructions for the specific transaction before joining a queue.

When should I stop using a general guide and hire help?

Get licensed help for a BIR notice, audit, assessment, VAT, estate or corporate tax, several missing periods, lost records, disputed income, possible fraud exposure, or a payment that the BIR system does not recognize.

Safest next steps

  1. List every tax type on your Certificate of Registration or BIR record.
  2. Gather returns, filing confirmations, payment references, Forms 2316/2307, and notices.
  3. Check which periods are actually missing before filing duplicates.
  4. Use current official forms and payment instructions.
  5. Request a professional computation when several periods or notices are involved.
